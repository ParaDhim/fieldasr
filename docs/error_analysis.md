# Error analysis

Model analysed: Whisper-small + LoRA, seed 0 (`results/whisper_lora_s0`), 487 test clips x 9
conditions. A "failure" is a clip with item WER >= 0.5. Everything below is generated by
`uv run python -m fieldasr.analysis.errors` into `results/error_analysis/`.

> **Status: the taxonomy is rule-based and the human listening pass is not done yet.**
> The tags are heuristics over reference/hypothesis text (see `tag_item`). The 100-clip
> stratified sample is in `results/error_analysis/listening_sheet.html` (audio players, reference,
> model output, auto tag, and a blank column for your own label). Replace the "auto" claims below
> with your labels after listening.

## 1. The dominant failure is not "wrong words" - it depends on the condition
Error decomposition (% of reference words), LoRA s0 vs XLS-R CTC s0:

| condition | LoRA sub | del | **ins** | WER | XLS-R sub | **del** | ins | WER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 17.0 | 2.0 | 2.1 | 21.0 | 18.5 | 2.3 | 2.1 | 22.9 |
| mp3_8k | 22.3 | 2.2 | 2.5 | 27.0 | 22.9 | 2.5 | 2.4 | 27.9 |
| amr_nb_8k | 33.0 | 2.3 | **13.3** | 48.6 | 33.3 | 5.8 | 2.5 | 41.5 |
| pink_snr0 | 53.8 | 4.6 | **37.8** | 96.1 | 47.5 | **24.3** | 1.8 | 73.6 |
| babble_snr0 | 67.3 | 3.6 | **66.9** | 137.8 | 77.3 | 2.4 | 28.0 | 107.6 |

* On clean and mildly degraded audio, errors are ~85% substitutions: the usual acoustic/lexical
  mistakes.
* Under heavy noise and phone-codec audio, **Whisper's WER is mostly insertions**. At babble 0 dB
  it emits about as many spurious words as there are reference words. The CTC model fails the
  other way (24% deletions at pink 0 dB: it falls silent). Different failure modes need different
  fixes: decoder-side guards for Whisper, acoustic robustness for CTC.
* Whisper under babble sometimes **transcribes the interfering talker** (a hallucination-tagged
  output repeated a phrase from another clip's speech), so "hallucination" there partly means
  "followed the wrong speaker". Our babble comes from the same corpus, which makes this easy to
  provoke.

Caveats on the tags: `proper_noun` fires whenever *any* capitalised mid-sentence reference
word is missed, so it is inflated on long Wikipedia-style sentences (it is still the main
*clean-audio* failure: 15 of 29). `noise_or_channel` is the residual label for any other failure in
a degraded condition, so it says "we cannot attribute this to a specific cause", not "noise
caused it". `speaker_or_unclear` is what is left on clean audio (7 clips); FLEURS has no accent
labels so **accent errors cannot be separated out**. Code-switching is only detectable as a
weak `foreign_token` heuristic (30 hits, not reported above): FLEURS Swahili is monolingual text
with English-origin names.

## 3. Representative failures (from the 100-clip sample)
* **English-origin names/terms (clean, the biggest clean-audio class):**
  REF `bomu moja lililipuka nje ya ofisi ya gavana mkuu` -> HYP `Bumu moja lini ni puka nje ya
  ofisi ya government kuu.` (the model "translates" a loanword back to English).
  REF `aerosmith wameghairi ...` -> `Aero Simitho Megairi ...`; REF `t. rex` -> `Trezeg`;
  REF `corps of engineers cha marekani` -> `Corp. Ingenia Chang Marekani`.
* **Numerals:** REF `mwaka wa 2009` -> HYP `mwaka walisi 2 na 9.` (digit tokenisation + AMR codec);
  REF `inchi 6 za mvua` -> lost entirely under 10 dB babble.
* **Word segmentation / orthography:** `unaotoka` vs `unatoka`-type near misses, `yakipekee` vs
  `ya kipekee`: CER is low but WER counts the whole word. Agglutinative Swahili punishes WER
  more than CER; CER should be reported next to WER (it is).
* **Hallucination loops:** 0 dB babble outputs like `Funganya boda mbili kaunishwa, ... funganya
  boda mbili kaunisha ...` repeated for the whole clip, unrelated to the target sentence.

## 4. What I would fix next
1. **Decoder guards for Whisper:** a no-speech / compression-ratio gate and a repetition penalty
   or max-length-from-duration cap. Cheap, and attacks the largest error mass at low SNR.
2. **Train with the field augmentations from the start** (the `whisper_full_aug` run already helps
   cheap-mic+reverb 55.9 -> 47.8 and babble 10 dB 43.1 -> 38.8 WER, one seed); add real noise
   recordings and real handset codecs instead of synthetic ones; add a 0-5 dB regime.
3. **Loanword / English-name handling:** add Swahili-spelled English names to training text, or a
   post-hoc lexicon; decide the policy (transcribe as spoken vs. canonical English spelling).
4. **Numerals:** train with a consistent numeral policy (digits vs words) and normalise both sides.
5. **Real accent / age / handset metadata** so the accent bucket can be measured.

## 5. What didn't work / caveats
* **Common Voice** (first choice) is gone from the HF hub; FLEURS has no speaker IDs, so splits
  could not be audited at speaker level.
* **First QC thresholds were wrong**: absolute -50 dB silence flagged 12% of clips falsely; and
  "duplicate text" is normal in FLEURS. Both were fixed after looking at the distributions.
* **Field augmentation did not rescue the hardest conditions** (pink/babble 0 dB stay ~95-130% WER
  for every Whisper variant): it helps moderate degradation, not extreme.
* **LoRA beat full fine-tuning on clean audio** (21.4 vs 25.2) - I did not test why (plausible:
  less forgetting / over-fitting on 13 h; unverified). The two are within noise at 0 dB.
* **wav2vec2** has one seed; its apparent robustness advantage at 0 dB and phone codec is
  a single-run observation, while its cheap-mic+reverb result is the worst of all systems.
* The auto-tagger has not been validated against human labels.
